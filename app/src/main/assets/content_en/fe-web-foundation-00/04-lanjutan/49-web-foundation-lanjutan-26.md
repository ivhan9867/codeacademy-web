What a journey! From simple paragraphs all the way to forms that can accept user data. Let's summarize everything before moving on to the quiz.

## Key takeaways

- **Paragraphs and Headings**: `<p>` for general text, `<h1>` through `<h6>` for hierarchical titles that build the page outline
- **Lists**: `<ul>` for unordered lists, `<ol>` for ordered lists (with `type`, `start`, and `reversed` attributes), `<dl>`/`<dt>`/`<dd>` for term-and-description pairs
- **Images**: `<img>` with `src` and `alt` attributes, sized via `width` or `height` (just one of them)
- **Block text formatting**: `<blockquote>` for long quotes, `<pre>` for text that preserves spaces and line breaks as written, `<figure>`/`<figcaption>` for self-contained content with its caption
- **Inline text formatting**: `<a>` for links, `<em>`/`<strong>` for emphasis, `<q>` for short quotes, `<cite>` for work references, `<dfn>` for term definitions, `<sub>`/`<sup>` for subscript and superscript, `<mark>` for highlights, `<br>` for line breaks
- **Semantic HTML**: `<header>`, `<footer>`, `<main>`, `<nav>` for high-level page structure; `<article>`, `<aside>`, `<section>` for more specific, meaningful content
- **Generic elements**: `<div>` (block) and `<span>` (inline) for grouping without special semantic meaning
- **Tables**: `<table>`, `<tr>`, `<td>`/`<th>` as the basic structure, `colspan`/`rowspan` for spanning cells, plus `caption`, `thead`/`tbody`/`tfoot`, `scope`, and `headers` for cleaner, more accessible tables
- **Forms**: `<input>` with various `type` values, `<textarea>` for long text, `<label>` as captions, attributes like `name`, `required`, `placeholder`, `pattern`, and others for validation, plus `<form>` with `action` and `method` to submit data
- **Special characters**: character references (`&name;` or `&#number;`) to display characters that have special meaning in HTML or symbols beyond basic characters
